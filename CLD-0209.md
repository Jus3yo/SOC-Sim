# CLD-0209: Unfamiliar sign-in properties: ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 3 · 2026-08-27
Verdict: Escalate · Severity (my rating): High (reported Low)
QA review: Excellent

## Alert
Rule 'Unfamiliar sign-in properties (guest account)' caused by ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io signed in at 10:55 UTC from 192.0.2.146 (hosting provider range, no prior history), Windows 10, Chrome. No MFA challenge issued under the guest policy. The session accessed Bexley-Integration SharePoint site and opened 34 documents in the Payroll-Mapping library, including files not touched since project setup. The account's prior baseline is evening sign-ins from 198.51.100.77 on macOS with single-document access.


## Hypothesis
Malicious: Guest account credentials without MFA challenge were compromised and used by an unauthorized party from a different infrastructure to access and exfiltrate sensitive payroll files.
Benign: Legitimate contractor performing authorized work from a new remote device, operating system, and IP range outside her usual working schedule.

## Evidence checked
KQL baseline query (signin | where User == 'ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io' | summarize count() by IP, Device, Client) confirms 4 historical sign-ins exclusively from 198.51.100.77 (London, UK) using macOS/Safari, contrasted against a single 1-off sign-in from 192.0.2.146 on Windows/Chrome;
Sign-in log CLD-0209 confirms no MFA challenge issued;
Proxy egress log CLD-0209 shows 34 document accesses in the Bexley Payroll-Mapping library within 25 minutes of login.

## Action
Escalated immediately to vCiso per Escalation SOP for confirmed access to client/payroll data.

## Justification
Escalating immediately because the Escalation SOP mandates for vCiso escalation upon confirmed access to client or payroll data. Five separate attribute changes at once: residential -> hosting provider, IP/city, operating system, browser, and time of day(evening to morning). Document access also jumped from single document reads to 34 payroll files in 25 minutes. Due to the guest accounts having no MFA policy enforced, there is no way to stop somebody who has the password to guest accounts.
